Creating a Secure Partner Portal for Primary Care Coalition

A healthcare UX capstone project focused on improving secure collaboration and document sharing between PCC staff and external partners.

View On Figma

Timeline

September 2025 – May 2026

Role

UX Researcher & Usability Testing Lead

Tools

Figma, SharePoint, Miro, Trello

Overview

Primary Care Coalition (PCC) is a nonprofit healthcare organization serving more than 30,000 uninsured and underserved individuals.

PCC needed a more secure and efficient way for staff and external healthcare partners to share documents and collaborate using SharePoint.

The Problem

Expired SharePoint Links

Staff repeatedly re-shared files because links expired unexpectedly.

Permission Complexity

Access management was confusing and often required IT support.

Security Concerns

Sensitive documents were shared through insecure email workflows.

Research

Before designing the Secure Partner Portal, we needed to understand PCC's existing workflows, stakeholders, and technical constraints. Our research combined organizational analysis, interviews, competitive analysis, and usability testing to identify opportunities for improving secure document sharing.

Background Research

We reviewed PCC's organizational structure, strategic planning documents, SharePoint environment, and current document-sharing workflow. This helped us understand:

  • How staff currently collaborated
  • Existing SharePoint limitations
  • Security requirements for healthcare data
  • Different user groups within PCC

This research allowed our team to begin the project with a strong understanding of PCC's organizational goals before proposing any design solutions.

Information Architecture

We organized the portal around four major program areas:

  • Administrative
  • Population Health
  • Healthcare Access
  • About PCC

The system used a two-tier model:

  • Public documents accessible without authentication
  • Private documents protected through authenticated access

Stakeholder Interviews

We interviewed PCC staff members who regularly shared documents with healthcare partners. Our interview questions focused on:

  • Current document-sharing workflows
  • Pain points using SharePoint
  • Permission management
  • Collaboration with external partners
  • Desired improvements

Several recurring themes emerged. Staff frequently mentioned spending time managing permissions, resending expired links, and answering questions about locating documents.

Affinity Diagramming

Competitive analysis comparing healthcare partner portals and document sharing workflows.

After interviews, our team organized dozens of observations into affinity groups. We clustered related comments to identify major themes across participants.

Key Themes

  • Difficulty finding documents
  • Confusing permissions
  • Need for centralized resources
  • Lack of consistency
  • Communication inefficiencies

These themes directly informed the information architecture and navigation for the final portal.

Usability Testing

I helped conduct usability testing sessions using our high-fidelity prototype. Participants completed realistic tasks while thinking aloud, allowing us to observe where they hesitated, became confused, or completed tasks successfully.

Sample Tasks

  • Locate a healthcare resource
  • Access a restricted document
  • Find program-specific information
  • Navigate between public and private content

Key Findings

  • Folder-based navigation increased confidence.
  • Participants preferred permanent folder links over individual document links.
  • Clear permission indicators reduced uncertainty.
  • Simplified navigation improved task completion.

Outcome

The project delivered:

  • Research-backed portal architecture
  • Validated mid and high-fidelity prototypes
  • Governance recommendations
  • SharePoint implementation guidance

What I Learned

This capstone project taught me how to balance user needs, security requirements, organizational goals, and technical constraints within a real healthcare environment.

I also learned that usability testing is about observing behavior rather than collecting opinions, and that information architecture directly shapes user confidence and workflow efficiency.
