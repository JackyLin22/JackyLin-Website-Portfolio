Improving the UMD Football Fan Experience

A UX research project exploring how undergraduate students experience UMD football games and identifying opportunities to improve engagement, attendance, and retention.

View Presentation PDF View Research Report

Timeline

September 2024 - December 2024

Role

UX Researcher

Methods

Contextual Inquiry, Ethnography, Affinity Mapping, Personas, Decision Point Models

Overview

As part of a UX research course at the University of Maryland, my team investigated why students attend football games, what keeps them engaged, and what causes them to leave early.

Our goal was to understand how UMD Athletics could improve the undergraduate fan experience through research-driven design solutions.

Research

Before proposing solutions, our team wanted to understand what motivated students to attend University of Maryland football games and what caused them to leave early. Rather than relying on assumptions, we combined multiple qualitative research methods to capture students' behaviors, motivations, and experiences throughout game day.

Our research included contextual inquiry interviews, ethnographic observations during a live football game, interpretation sessions, affinity diagramming, and decision point modeling. Using multiple methods allowed us to compare observations across participants and identify recurring patterns instead of isolated opinions.

Contextual Inquiry Interviews

We interviewed 21 undergraduate students representing different majors, class years, and levels of football knowledge. Rather than asking only whether students enjoyed football games, we asked participants to walk us through their most recent game-day experience from beginning to end.

Questions focused on topics such as:

  • How they decided to attend a game
  • Who they attended with
  • How they spent time before kickoff
  • What influenced them to stay or leave
  • What made the experience enjoyable or frustrating

Across interviews, students consistently described football games as social events. Many attended because their friends were going, while others admitted they rarely stayed for the entire game if their group decided to leave.

Ethnographic Observation

To complement interviews, our team attended a live Maryland football game to observe student behavior in context. We documented how students entered the stadium, interacted with friends, participated during the game, and responded to halftime activities.

We observed that many students were highly engaged before kickoff and during major plays, but engagement noticeably declined as the game progressed. Groups frequently left together around halftime, particularly when the score became one-sided or when friends wanted to continue their evening elsewhere.

These observations helped validate themes that emerged during interviews and revealed behaviors participants had not explicitly described.

Key Findings

Students Attended for Social Reasons

Many participants viewed football games primarily as social events rather than sporting events.

Students Frequently Left at Halftime

Long game durations, fatigue, expensive concessions, and low engagement contributed to early departures.

Halftime Entertainment Was Underutilized

Students enjoyed the band and traditions, but many felt disconnected from halftime activities.

Football Knowledge Affected Engagement

Students unfamiliar with football rules struggled to stay invested in the gameplay.

Design Recommendations

Each recommendation was directly informed by our research findings. Rather than redesigning the entire game-day experience, we focused on improving the moments where students were most likely to disengage.

Enhanced Halftime Experience

Because many students considered leaving during halftime, we proposed interactive activities including QR-code challenges, live polls, student competitions, and social media participation to encourage students to remain engaged until the second half.

Gamified Mobile Experience

To encourage repeat attendance and sustained engagement, we designed a concept for a mobile application featuring attendance rewards, digital badges, interactive trivia, live polls, and redeemable student incentives.

Outcome

Our research resulted in several recommendations that addressed the largest barriers to student engagement. The proposed halftime activities and gamified mobile experience were designed to encourage students to stay longer, participate more actively, and feel connected to the campus community throughout the game.

By grounding each recommendation in research findings, we demonstrated how user-centered design can improve both the student experience and attendance goals for UMD Athletics.

What I Learned

This project strengthened my understanding of how multiple research methods work together to create a more complete picture of user behavior. While interviews revealed students' perspectives, observing them during an actual football game uncovered behaviors and decision-making patterns that participants did not always mention themselves.

I also learned the importance of synthesizing large amounts of qualitative data into clear themes that can guide design decisions. Affinity diagramming and decision point modeling helped transform dozens of individual observations into actionable insights.

In future projects, I plan to spend even more time observing users in their natural environments, validating findings through multiple research methods, and ensuring every design recommendation can be directly traced back to user evidence. This project reinforced my belief that the strongest design decisions begin with understanding people rather than starting with solutions.
